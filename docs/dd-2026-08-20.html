Source, and what it is not

Company announcements were read through the owner's connected InvestorPA tool, queried by ticker. The pipeline gate on that host — the fetch layer pulling PDFs in bulk from a re-host whose terms nobody has read — is untouched. Nothing read here was written to canonical or derived tables, no identifiers were enumerated, DECLARED_SOURCES is unchanged. Adopting it for ingestion still needs the two open questions answered by a human.

Every figure below comes from the company's own lodged document, cited beneath the claim. There is still no price vendor, so there is no current market price anywhere in this note. Multiples are computed at the price the directors themselves paid, which is a disclosed fact.

BSA BSA Limited Signal falls

The screen ranked it fourth on conviction: David Geraghty, stake up 145%, $896,000 at $0.32 on 21 July — part of the largest single-director accumulation on the corpus, $1,199,913 across five notices in nineteen days, paying up from $0.30 for the biggest parcel. On trade mechanics alone it is the most emphatic thing on the screen.

What a 3Y cannot tell you

the Company has executed a loan agreement with an entity associated with the Company's Non-Executive Chair, David Geraghty … Loan Amount: up to A$2,000,000 … Purpose: The Loan Facility may be drawn only for the purpose of acquiring ordinary shares in the capital of the Company by way of an on-market acquisition and/or off-market share transfer.

BSA Limited — Grant of Loan Facility to Director, 9 June 2026 (price sensitive)

Unsecured, 10% per annum, three-year term, no repayment for the first twelve months. The borrower is Mandarin Rock Pty Ltd — the third vehicle named in the notice still sitting in the platform's review queue — with Geraghty and Beibei Yu guaranteeing it jointly and severally.

It was drawn. The June-quarter cash flow statement carries the line item in plain words, as an investing outflow, disclosed again as a related-party payment:

Director's loan to acquire BSA shares  (2.0)

BSA Limited — Appendix 4C, quarter ended 30 June 2026

Drawn before the July buying began. Geraghty is on the hook personally through the guarantee, so this is not a free option and should not be described as one — but the capital is the company's, lent unsecured, and the screen's whole premise, a director committing his own money, does not survive it. Some earlier buying predates the facility. The $896,000 parcel does not.

Verdict

Not a director-conviction signal. That may be a legitimate special situation for someone who wants one. It is not what the screen said, and anyone reading the screen alone would have had it exactly backwards.

SPZ Smart Parking Limited Signal holds

The only name on both screens with material dollars: $2,079,725 across two directors on 19 August, no counter-evidence, complete document coverage. Diligence strengthened it rather than weakening it, on three counts that a 3Y cannot carry.

They bought the day after results

The Appendix 4E, the investor presentation and the buy-back announcement all lodged on 18 August. Both directors bought on the 19th — with the full-year numbers public and the window open, not ahead of them. They also paid different prices, $0.8121 and $0.8239, so these were two independent orders rather than one block split between them.

The company was buying too

A $5.0m on-market buy-back was announced the same morning, to commence 1 September for up to twelve months — described by the CEO as a "signal of confidence in our outlook".

And so was a specialist institution

Microequities Asset Management lodged a Form 604 on 20 August taking it from 13.82% to 14.97%, 57,656,892 shares, with changes running to 19 August. That is corroboration of a different kind from a director notice, and the screens have no channel for it.

The risks that actually matter

  • UK concentration against live regulatory risk. The UK is 67% of revenue and 68% of breach notices. A Government Code of Practice consultation opened in July 2025. Denmark is the worked example of what that does: the Danish Ministry of Transport banned ANPR-issued initial notices from 1 July 2025, and Danish revenue went $1.3m → $0.3m, notices −53%, EBITDA −$2.9m. The same event in the UK is not a haircut, it is most of group earnings. The company says current UK proposals are "more favourable" than the withdrawn 2022 code and that timing "remains uncertain" — which is honest, and is not the same as safe.
  • UK growth was collections, not volume. UK notices rose 4%; revenue per ticket rose 54%. About 750,000 aged notices sit in the resolution process with eighteen months or more to work through. That is a stock being converted, not a flow.
  • Effective tax rate 15.7% → 40.7%.
  • Currency. A stronger Australian dollar cost $2.9m of revenue and $1.0m of EBITDA, on top of $2.7m of FX losses. Sixty-seven per cent of revenue is sterling.
  • Serial acquisition. Goodwill of $40.6m within $59.0m of intangibles, against $98.9m of equity, from six deals since 2021.
  • UNPATA is non-IFRS and unaudited, and the Appendix 4E accounts are "in the process of being audited".
Worth naming rather than burying

The directors bought on 19 August, about two weeks before the company's own $5.0m buy-back begins on 1 September. That is after full disclosure and unremarkable in itself — but the sequence, results then buy-back announcement then director buying then company buying, is worth seeing whole rather than as one row on a screen.

Verdict

The best-evidenced buying signal on the screen. Same-day independent purchases at different prices, immediately post-results, corroborated by a buy-back and by a specialist institution moving to roughly 15%. A real, growing, cash-generative business — priced as one, with a concentration risk that is genuine and already demonstrated next door in Denmark.

Two things this exposed in the platform

Every actionable date on both screens was early

actionable_from is the lodgement date, and for 971 of the 1,109 dated documents held — 88% — that timestamp came from the PDF's creation time. Creation is when the company made the notice, not when the exchange released it. Proven exactly on SPZ:

Our lodged_at (docs 557/558)19 Aug 2026, 20:20 AEST
Actual ASX release20 Aug 2026, 08:00 AEST

Eleven hours forty minutes early, and on the wrong calendar day — the screen said those rows were actionable on the 19th when nobody could act before the 20th. Across five BSA notices the skew runs from two minutes to nearly three days, always the same direction, landing on the wrong day whenever a notice was prepared after the close or over a weekend. It applied to all 28 signal rows.

This is look-ahead bias in the column whose entire purpose is preventing it. It cannot be fixed from the data held: the true release time is not in it, and the source that has it is not declared. Both screens now carry actionable_from_may_be_early so they stop asserting a date they cannot support. A flag is not a fix. The fix is capturing a release timestamp at detection, and it deserves priority — until then every forward test the platform runs is flattered by up to a trading day.

BSA is the argument for reading beyond the 3Y

The loan announcement is price-sensitive, sits in the same company's announcement stream, and inverts the meaning of the trade. The screens have no channel for related-party financing of a purchase because they only read 3Ys. Worth building: flag conviction rows where the entity has lodged a price-sensitive related-party or financial-assistance notice in the preceding ninety days.

Three of nineteen conviction rows do not mean what the ordering implies. No position is recommended here and no price is forecast — this is the evidence, and what it does and does not support.
