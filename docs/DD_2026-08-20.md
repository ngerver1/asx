# Due diligence — the 20 August 2026 director screens

Worked from the screens' own output: 19 conviction rows, 9 cluster rows, on a
corpus of 1,124 documents and 311 canonical trades. The screens rank on
disclosed trade mechanics alone, which is all they hold. This note is what
happens when the three highest-ranked names are checked against the companies'
own announcements.

The headline: **the screen's #4 conviction row is an artefact of a company
loan, and the screen had no way to know.** That is the finding worth acting on.

## Source and its limits

Company announcements were read through the owner's connected InvestorPA tool,
queried by ticker. `docs/SOURCE_INVESTORPA.md` gates investorpa.com as a
*pipeline* source — the fetch layer pulling PDFs in bulk from a re-host whose
terms nobody has read — and that gate is untouched. Nothing read here was
written to canonical or derived tables, no identifiers were enumerated, and
`DECLARED_SOURCES` is unchanged. Adopting it for ingestion still needs the two
questions in that document answered by a human.

Every figure below is from the company's own lodged document, cited by date.
There is still no price vendor (ACCESS_DECISION §3), so there is no current
market price anywhere in this note. Multiples are computed at the price the
directors themselves paid, which is a disclosed fact, and are labelled as such.

## BSA — the signal does not survive

**Screen said:** conviction row 4. David Geraghty, +145%, $896,000 at $0.32 on
21 July, the largest single-director accumulation on the corpus at $1,199,913
across five notices in nineteen days, paying up from $0.30 for the biggest
parcel.

**What the screen could not see** (BSA announcement, 9 June 2026, price
sensitive):

> the Company has executed a loan agreement with an entity associated with the
> Company's Non-Executive Chair, David Geraghty … Loan Amount: up to
> A$2,000,000 … **Purpose: The Loan Facility may be drawn only for the purpose
> of acquiring ordinary shares in the capital of the Company** by way of an
> on-market acquisition and/or off-market share transfer.

Unsecured, 10% p.a., three-year term, no repayment for the first 12 months.
Borrower is Mandarin Rock Pty Ltd — the third vehicle named in the notice our
review queue is holding — with Geraghty and Beibei Yu guaranteeing it jointly
and severally.

The facility was drawn. The Appendix 4C for the quarter ended 30 June carries
the line item verbatim:

> Director's loan to acquire BSA shares (2.0)

classified as an investing cash outflow, disclosed again at item 6.2 as a
related-party payment. It was drawn before the July buying began.

Geraghty is on the hook personally through the guarantee, so this is not a free
option and should not be described as one. But the *capital* is the company's,
lent unsecured, and the screen's entire premise — a director committing his own
money — does not hold. Some of his earlier buying (March, May, 11 June notices)
predates the facility; the $896,000 parcel does not.

**And the business behind it** (Appendix 4C 30 July; Appendix 4E and Annual
Report 20 August):

| | FY26 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $32.1m | $286.8m (**−89%**) |
| Underlying EBITDA | $6.4m | $21.2m |
| Q4 reported EBITDA | −$2.1m | — |
| Operating cash flow | −$4,443k | +$30,475k |
| Cash | $16,822k | $23,968k |
| Net assets | $13,851k | $6,953k |
| Debt | nil | nil |

Revenue fell 89% on the expiry of the nbn contract and lost Bluecurrent and
Intellihub tenders. Q4 underlying EBITDA was $0.3m — the FY26 $6.4m includes
non-recurring transition-out payments. Shares on issue 75,300,000, unchanged.

At Geraghty's $0.32 that is a **$24.1m market cap against $16.4m net cash** and
a $2.0m related-party receivable: roughly $5.7m for the operating rump plus
whatever the acquisition mandate is worth. The annual report is explicit that
the mandate is the strategy — "ongoing engagement with our advisers to
identify, evaluate, and assess potential acquisition candidates" — and a mining
executive joined the board in March 2026. An on-market buy-back was announced
30 April.

**Verdict.** Not a director-conviction signal. It is a cash box with a shrinking
services rump, hunting a reverse takeover, supporting its own stock through two
company-funded channels at once — a buy-back and a loan to its Chair. That may
be a legitimate special situation for someone who wants that; it is not what
the screen said, and anyone reading the screen alone would have had it exactly
backwards. **Downgrade to the bottom of the list.**

## SPZ — the signal survives, and gets stronger

**Screen said:** the only name on both screens with material dollars.
$2,079,725 across two directors on 19 August, no counter-evidence, complete
document coverage.

**What the announcements add — all three point the same way:**

1. **They bought the day after FY26 results.** The Appendix 4E, investor
   presentation and buy-back announcement all lodged 18 August; both directors
   bought on the 19th. They were buying with the full-year numbers public and
   the trading window open, not ahead of them.
2. **The company was buying too.** A $5.0m on-market buy-back was announced the
   same morning, to commence 1 September for up to 12 months, described by the
   CEO as a "signal of confidence in our outlook".
3. **A specialist institution was buying alongside.** Microequities Asset
   Management lodged a Form 604 on 20 August taking it from 13.82% to
   **14.97%** — 57,656,892 shares — with changes running to 19 August. That is
   independent corroboration of a different kind from a director notice.

**FY26 (year to 30 June 2026, unaudited):**

| | FY26 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $126.0m | $77.2m (+63%) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $30.8m | $20.5m (+50%) |
| EBITDA (statutory) | $25.9m | $18.5m |
| NPAT | $6.9m | $5.4m (+28%) |
| UNPATA (non-IFRS) | $11.4m | $6.6m (+73%) |
| Adjusted free cash flow | $20.0m | $12.8m |
| Cash (ex client funds) | $17.4m | $12.7m |
| Total equity | $98.9m | $87.8m |
| ANPR sites | 2,083 | 1,799 |

The adjusted-EBITDA bridge is not aggressive: $25.9m statutory plus $1.8m
acquisition professional fees, $0.4m other non-recurring and $2.7m FX losses.
72% of revenue growth was organic.

**At the $0.8124 the directors paid**, on 420,631,770 shares: market cap
**$341.7m**; EV about **$331m** after $17.4m cash and the $7.2m drawn on 1 July
for the American Parking acquisition. That is **~10.8× EV/adjusted EBITDA**,
**~30× UNPATA**, **~49× statutory earnings**, 3.5× book. Not a cheap stock. The
question this DD can answer is whether the buying is well-founded, not whether
the price is.

**FY27, on management's own stated numbers** (presentation p.19):

| | FY26 | FY27 guided | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK enhanced debt resolution | +$7.0m | +$5.0m | **−$2.0m** |
| Denmark EBITDA | −$2.9m | −$1.5m | +$1.4m |
| Switzerland EBITDA | −$3.8m | c.−$2.5m | +$1.3m |
| Germany | −$1.0m | breakeven ~190 sites during FY27 | up to +$1.0m |
| American Parking | — | US$1.4m EBITDA (2025 basis) | +~A$2.1m |

Plus 450–600 net new ANPR sites targeted and the annualisation of the 303 added
in H2 FY26. The much-quoted debt-resolution headwind is real and
management-flagged, and on their own numbers it is outweighed by the loss-making
territories improving. These are company forecasts, not verified facts.

**The risks that actually matter:**

- **UK concentration against live regulatory risk.** The UK is 67% of revenue
  and 68% of PBNs. A Government Code of Practice consultation opened July 2025.
  **Denmark is the worked example of what that does**: the Danish Ministry of
  Transport banned ANPR-issued initial PBNs from 1 July 2025 and Danish revenue
  went $1.3m → $0.3m, PBNs −53%, EBITDA −$2.9m. The same event in the UK is not
  a haircut, it is most of group earnings. The 4E says current UK proposals are
  "more favourable" than the withdrawn 2022 code and that timing "remains
  uncertain" — which is honest, and is not the same as safe.
- **UK growth was collections, not volume.** UK PBNs rose 4%; revenue per ticket
  rose 54%. About 750,000 aged PBNs are in the resolution process with 18+
  months to work through. That is a stock being converted, not a flow.
- Effective tax rate 15.7% → 40.7%.
- FX: a stronger AUD cost $2.9m of revenue and $1.0m of EBITDA, on top of $2.7m
  of FX losses. 67% of revenue is GBP.
- Goodwill $40.6m of $59.0m intangibles against $98.9m equity, from six
  acquisitions since 2021. Serial-acquirer integration and impairment risk.
- UNPATA is non-IFRS and unaudited; the 4E accounts are "in the process of being
  audited".

**One thing to name rather than bury.** The directors bought on 19 August, about
two weeks before the company's own $5.0m buy-back begins on 1 September. That
is after full disclosure and unremarkable in itself, but the sequence — results,
buy-back announcement, director buying, then company buying — is worth seeing
whole rather than as one row on a screen.

**Verdict.** The best-evidenced buying signal on the screen, and the DD
strengthened rather than weakened it: same-day independent purchases at
different prices, immediately post-results, corroborated by a buy-back and by a
specialist institution moving to ~15%. It is a real, growing, cash-generative
business. It is priced as one, and the concentration risk is genuine and
demonstrated next door in Denmark.

## CBE — the alternative worth keeping

Cobre Ltd, copper — Kalahari (Botswana) and Sierra Atacama (Chile). Cluster row:
$236,218 across Martin Holland and Michael Addison.

Classification checked against source text, because a A$90M institutional raise
completed on 9 July sits right beside these dates and placement participation
misread as on-market buying would have been the obvious trap. It is not:
Holland's notice reads "On market purchase", both Addison notices "On market
acquisition". All three genuinely on-market.

- Holland, 13 July: +376,117 at $0.265 ($99,666), from 15,447,434 held
- Addison, 4 Aug: +377,377 at $0.285 ($107,552), to a round 7,000,000
- Addison, 5 Aug: +100,000 at $0.29 ($29,000)

Both were already large holders, and **Addison paid up after the raise** — the
$90M was placed on 9 July, and he bought on-market at $0.285–0.29 in August,
above Holland's July price. Surrounding announcements: Sierra Atacama declared
first positive cash flow (6 July), Cobre increased its ownership of it (7 July),
**Rothschild & Co appointed strategic adviser** (10 August), MSCI Global Micro
Cap Index inclusion (13 August), EGM called (18 August).

A strategic-adviser appointment plus an EGM is a corporate-action shape, and
index inclusion brings mechanical buying. Entirely different risk profile from
SPZ — resource development, and the $90M raise means the dilution has already
happened.

**Note for the corpus:** a third CBE director notice (Michael McNeilly, 13 July)
is not held. The cluster may be larger than two.

## Two things this exposed in the platform

**1. Every actionable date on both screens is early.** `actionable_from` is
`lodged_at`'s date, and for 971 of 1,109 dated documents (88%) `lodged_at` came
from the PDF's creation time. Creation is when the company *made* the notice,
not when ASX released it. Proven exactly on SPZ:

| | |
|---|---|
| our `lodged_at` (docs 557/558) | 19 Aug 2026 20:20 AEST |
| actual ASX release | 20 Aug 2026 08:00 AEST |

11h40m early, and on the wrong calendar day — the screen said those rows were
actionable on the 19th when nobody could act before the 20th. Across five BSA
notices the skew runs from 2 minutes to nearly 3 days, always the same
direction, landing on the wrong day whenever the notice was prepared after the
close or over a weekend. It applied to **all 28 signal rows**.

This is look-ahead bias in the column whose entire purpose is preventing it. It
cannot be fixed from the data held — the true release time is not in it, and the
source that has it is not declared. Both screens now carry
`actionable_from_may_be_early` so they stop asserting a date they cannot
support. **The real fix is capturing a release timestamp at detection**, and it
should be prioritised: it silently flatters every forward test the platform will
ever run.

**2. The BSA row is the argument for `counter_evidence` going further.** The
loan announcement is price-sensitive, is in the same company's announcement
stream, and inverts the meaning of the trade. The screen has no channel for
"related-party financing of the purchase" because it only reads 3Ys. Worth
considering: flag conviction rows where the entity has lodged a price-sensitive
related-party or financial-assistance notice in the preceding 90 days.
